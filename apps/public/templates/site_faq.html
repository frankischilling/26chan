What is 26chan?

An imageboard organized by topic. Public discussion does not require a registered account. Each board has its own posting and media settings.

How do I post?

Open a board and use the new-thread form, or open a thread to reply. Where uploads are available, upload your file first and finish the post after processing succeeds. Text-only boards do not accept attachments.

How do I quote a post?

Type two greater-than signs followed by the post number, such as >>123. A cross-board reference uses >>>/board/123. Clicking a post number can fill the reply form.

How do spoilers work?

Where enabled, put text between [spoiler] and [/spoiler]. The image-spoiler option hides an attachment thumbnail until the reader chooses to view it.

What are tripcodes?

A tripcode distinguishes a name without registering it. Add # followed by a secret to the name field. Secure tripcodes use ## where configured. Keep the secret private.

What does sage do?

Put sage in the options field to reply without bumping the thread. Board limits can also prevent a thread from bumping.

How do I delete my post?

Use “Delete or report” on your post. Choose “File only” to remove just the attachment. Keep the browser session that created the post, or use the post's deletion password where available. Waiting periods, board restrictions and archived threads can prevent deletion.

How do I report a post?

Use the report form attached to the post. Reporting availability and categories depend on the board. A report does not automatically remove content.

Where do older threads go?

Boards with archives provide an Archive link. Archived threads are read-only and can expire. Deleted content is not available through the public interface.

What is the blotter?

The blotter contains short operator announcements. Some boards show its latest messages above the threads.

Where can I find more information?

Read the local rules, news and operator contact page. Features that need optional services are available only when those services are configured.
